# Restoring Literary Style in Adaptations for EFL Learners

This repository contains supplementary data for the paper:

> **Iglika Nikolova-Stoupak, Gaël Lejeune, and Eva Schaeffer-Lacroix.**  
> *Restoring Literary Style in Adaptations for EFL Learners.*  
> Innovation in Language Learning, Florence, Italy, 11–13 November 2026.

The paper investigates the restoration of literary style in texts adapted for learners of English as a Foreign Language (EFL), including an evaluation with EFL readers.

## Repository Contents

### `survey/`

This folder contains the anonymised data collected through the reader survey conducted as part of the study.

#### `survey_results.csv`

Contains the anonymised survey responses used in the analysis.

Information that could directly identify or contact participants, including email addresses and follow-up information, has been removed.

#### `survey_texts.pdf`

Contains the adapted texts used in the survey. 

Sources for the baseline adapted texts: 

Oscar Wilde, The Picture of Dorian Gray, adapted by L. A. Hill (B2), Oxford Progressive English Readers, Oxford University Press, 1994.
Oscar Wilde, The Picture of Dorian Gray, adapted byVictoria Heward (B1), Black Cat, CIDEB, 2020.
Herman Melville, Moby Dick, adapted by Kathy Burke (A2), Penguin Readers, Pearson, 2006.
Herman Melville, Moby Dick, adapted by Fiona Mackenzie (B2), Penguin Readers, Penguin Books, 2020.

#### `survey_questions.pdf`

Contains the questions and instructions included in the survey.

#### `what_happens_next_responses.txt`

Contains participants' free-text responses to the question:

> *What do you think will happen next?*

For each response, the file also provides the relevant experimental metadata:

- participant number;
- CEFR level;
- literary work;
- adaptation type;
- whether the participant correctly identified the literary work.

Participant numbers are anonymous identifiers used only to distinguish responses.

## Data Organisation

The survey compares three types of text:

- **Baseline** — professionally adapted version;
- **Enriched** — semi-automatically enriched version;
- **Style transfer** — automatically modified version using style transfer.

The survey includes excerpts from:

- *Moby-Dick* by Herman Melville;
- *The Picture of Dorian Gray* by Oscar Wilde.

The texts cover CEFR levels A2, B1, and B2.
